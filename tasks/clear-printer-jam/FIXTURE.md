# Physical build and reset for the rear paper path

Build this unpowered fixture in place of sourcing a specific used printer. The shell dimensions, full A4 sheet, guide positions and locked feed nip follow the submitted simulation. The [HP rear-access procedure](https://support.hp.com/gb-en/document/c06448951) explains the two-handed removal this setup reproduces. The fixture uses standard sheet stock, passive springs and two rubber-covered tubes. It needs no powered printer, toner or proprietary spare parts.

## Parts and dimensions

All dimensions are in millimetres. Set the origin at the nip centre projected onto the bare station table. Local X points into the printer, Y along the rollers and Z up. The origin is at station X 572, Y 0. The bare tabletop is Z 0. A negative local X is closer to the robot.

Use four M6 × 40 machine screws, four M6 nuts, plain M6 washers and four 6 mm ID × 5.5 mm axial spacers for the tubes. Add eight M4 × 18 flat-head wood screws for the cheek feet, four M4 × 16 self-tapping screws for the caps and two M4 × 35 adjustment screws with M4 nuts and 8 mm OD washers. Use ordinary wood screws for the shell and guide supports. The plates can be made from offcuts. The printed hardware is small enough for a desktop printer. The force gauge and caliper are shared build tools. Export parts by selecting `part` in OpenSCAD and exporting as STL, or run `openscad -o cheek.stl -D 'part="cheek"' fixture_parts.scad` for the cheek and repeat with the other part names.

## Assembly and calibration

1. Assemble the shell and guides from the dimensions above. Screw each cantilever to the base at X −24 and +10, through its centreline. Fit the 23 mm riser on the overhang and glue and screw it to the cantilever. Secure the assembly to the station so the rear guide is stable. Round the two exposed edges of each guide to about 1 mm radius and sand them smooth. Confirm a strip can slide through the whole path above the guides with the upper roller out.
2. Centre the printed cheeks at local X 0. The printed part has a base at its own Z 0, so cut matching recesses through the base under its two feet or place the cheeks in the two side cutouts before fixing the base. The cheek foot bottoms must sit on the station table. Use countersunk screws through the feet into the table mounting substrate or into a flush auxiliary mounting strip. Keep the inner guide face at Y ±120 and all supports outside that span. Check both lower axle holes are at Z 59 ± 0.5 and align across Y.
3. Fix the wrapped lower tube in the two Z 59 holes. Insert the M6 screws from the outer sides through washers and into the captive end-plug nuts. Add flat spacers to fill the 5.5 mm gap at each side between plug collar and cheek. Tighten so the roller cannot turn. Place the upper tube between the two sliding carriages the same way. Tighten its screws against the carriages. The square carriages prevent it from turning and must slide freely in the slots. File their sides as needed for free travel. With sleeves touching, check the nip is centred at X 0 ± 1, Z 71 ± 0.5, with both axes parallel across the 225 mm face. Index both rubber seams away from the nip.
4. Drop the upper roller and its two carriages in from above and place one spring in each recess. Press an M4 nut into the hex socket under each cap, or retain it there with a drop of epoxy kept clear of the threads. Fit the caps. Run each M4 preload screw through its nut and bond one 8 mm washer to the end of the screw as a spring seat. Start with equal settings and about 3 mm spring compression per side. Keep enough screw engagement to hold the seats and enough spring travel to lift the upper tube by at least 3 mm. Make this check before loading a sheet. The rollers should touch lightly across their width. The simulated spring and upper mass put about 1.6 N across the nip, so use the pull measurement below to account for the different mass and surface material of the built fixture.
5. Calibrate with a straight fresh A4 sheet. Insert the sheet centred at Y 0 through the nip. Grip its accessible end across the centre with a light ruler and two small binder clips, both clear of the guide. Connect a centred pull loop to a 0–5 N spring or digital force gauge with 0.1 N or finer graduations. Check gauge zero and, if needed, check with a 150 g test mass, which reads about 1.47 N. Pull horizontally at Z 71, 20 ± 5 mm/s, through at least 60 mm. Use a ruler and stopwatch to set that speed. Target **1.5–2.2 N during sliding** and **at most 2.8 N at breakaway**, for three fresh sheets in a row. This is a fabrication target based on the model's nominal nip load and two-sided rubber friction, rather than an experimental measurement of a specific printer. Adjust both spring screws by the same amount, in quarter-turns. If either side slips early, level the roller contact and recheck before increasing the preload. Record the three sliding and peak readings and the paper type. Recheck whenever the sleeves or paper lot change. Remove the calibration clips, pull loop and gauge before the trial.

## Form and load each sheet

1. Start with a fresh conditioned sheet from the calibration pack. Measure along its long dimension from the edge that will face the robot. Make small pencil ticks on both edges at 33 and 66 mm. No ticks are needed in the gripping area. Use a 2 mm radius rounded metal or hardwood edge and two straight pieces of scrap board to form across the full 210 mm width. The printed angle formers, or a protractor set to the same angles, provide repeatable stops. Bend the first 66 mm up 30° about the 66 mm line, then bend the first 33 mm another 42° about the 33 mm line. Use a rounded crease and press along the whole width evenly. Adjust overbend to account for springback, then verify the released profile in the next step.
2. Rest the flat part on a level surface 71 mm above the table. Use a square and ruler or the [full-scale curl template](curl-profile.svg) to check the shape at both Y edges. From the 66 mm mark, the 33 mm mark rises **16.5 ± 3 mm** and is **28.6 ± 3 mm** closer to the robot. The accessible end rises a further **31.4 ± 3 mm** and is **10.2 ± 3 mm** closer. Check the two ends of the free edge differ in height by at most 3 mm. Reject sheets with a tear, diagonal fold, or a curl outside those bounds.
3. Lift the upper roller by the carriages and load the flat end first. Set the trailing edge to **X +163 ± 3 mm** from the nip and centre the page on Y 0 within 2 mm. An erasable setup line on the inside guide provides the depth reference. The free end starts around **X −107 mm, Z 119 mm** (station X 465 mm). Use the **X −68 mm, Z 71 mm** position of the 66 mm crease as a second check. Lower the upper roller, refit the roof if removed, and remove all setup tools. Leave the bench clear in front of the fixture. The exposed curled end remains free, with no tab, clip or attachment on it.
4. After a trial, examine the open path for pieces and check the released sheet. Remove fragments before resetting. Replace the page for the next run and repeat the same depth and profile checks.

The fabrication geometry and force target are specified for reproducible setup. The submission's recordings are the native YAM simulation trials. No physical build or calibration measurements are reported here.
